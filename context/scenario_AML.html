Cash Structuring

Typology: Sub-threshold cash deposits to avoid TTR obligations
High Risk
Description

James Hartley (CBA-001), a former NSW state politician flagged as a PEP, makes three consecutive cash deposits across three days — $9,800, $9,500, and $9,200 — all just below the $10,000 TTR threshold. This pattern is a textbook structuring (also called "smurfing") red flag. No individual transaction triggers a mandatory TTR, but the combined pattern generates a UAR under the SUB-10K-STRUCTURING monitoring rule and escalates to an SMR and P2 case.

Sanctions Entity SWIFT Wire

Typology: Large outbound international transfer to sanctioned counterparty
Critical Risk
Description

Apex Capital Pty Ltd (CBA-003), a business customer classified as Critical risk with a confirmed UN Security Council 1267 sanctions match, sends two SWIFT wire transfers to UAE bank accounts — $2.4M and $850k — within 72 hours. The beneficiary accounts are linked to entities on the OFAC SDN list. Viktor Kozlov (CBA-004), Apex Capital's beneficial owner, is himself an OFAC SDN listed individual. The combined $3.25M outflow constitutes a potential sanctions breach and textbook layering pattern in the money laundering cycle.

PEP High-Value Transfers

Typology: Politically Exposed Person receiving large NPP payments of unknown origin
Critical Risk
Description

Viktor Kozlov (CBA-004) is classified as a Critical-risk PEP (foreign government official) and is listed on the OFAC SDN list. His account receives three high-value NPP transfers — $85,000, $42,000, and $110,000 — within 5 days, all from unknown origins inconsistent with his declared income profile. PEPs require Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) under Australia's AML/CTF Act. The unknown source of funds, combined with his sanctions status, creates a high-probability integration scenario in the money laundering cycle.

4

Post-Death Account Activity

Typology: Financial transactions occurring after a customer's recorded death date
Medium Risk
Description

Sarah Williamson (CBA-005) passed away on 2024-11-03. Despite this, her account continues to receive BECS credits and generate BPAY debits in the weeks following death. This pattern triggers the DECEASED-ACCOUNT-TXN monitoring rule. Post-death account activity can indicate identity misuse, estate fraud, or simply recurring automated payments that haven't been cancelled. The bank must verify the death, contact next of kin, restrict the account, and determine the nature of each post-death transaction.

5

Threshold Transaction Reporting

Typology: Mandatory TTR filing vs. deliberate structuring to avoid TTR obligations
Compliance Check
Description

This scenario contrasts two cases: (a) a clean customer making a legitimate $10,000 cash deposit that correctly triggers a TTR filing, and (b) a PEP customer making sub-$10k deposits specifically to avoid that obligation. Mei Lin Chen (CBA-002) deposits exactly $10,000 in cash — this is straightforward and the TTR was filed within 10 business days as required by AUSTRAC. James Hartley (Scenario 1) deliberately stayed under $10k. This scenario tests whether the TTR file contains the right rows and whether the structuring pattern is captured in the UAR/case pipeline instead.
